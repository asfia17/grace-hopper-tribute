Grace Hopper

The woman who helped make programming accessible to the world.

HER CONTRIBUTION

From Machines to Modern Programming

Grace Brewster Murray Hopper was an American computer scientist and United States Navy rear admiral whose work helped shape the foundations of modern programming.

At a time when computers were difficult to program and largely understood through machine-oriented instructions, Hopper believed programming could become more accessible.

1944

Joined the Harvard Mark I Project

Hopper worked on the Harvard Mark I, one of the earliest electromechanical computers.

1952

Developed an Early Compiler

Her work on compiler technology helped demonstrate that computers could work with instructions closer to human language.

1959

Contributed to COBOL

Hopper played an influential role in the development and adoption of COBOL, a programming language designed for business applications.

LEGACY

Inspired Generations of Developers

Her emphasis on practical programming, education and innovation continues to influence the computing community.

Why Her Work Matters
